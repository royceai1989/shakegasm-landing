Long-Distance Phone Games For Couples (Beyond Trivia)

Distance does not kill a relationship. A boring weekly call does. Two people on FaceTime at 9pm Sunday, staring past each other, running out of status updates by minute six. A phone game sitting next to that call turns six awkward minutes into an hour that both people put on their calendar.

The common fix is trivia, and trivia is a trap for couples. One person wins by knowing more state capitals, the other loses by trying to care. A long distance couples game has a different job. It builds laughter density, keeps both players equally bad at it, and runs in fifteen minutes without burning the phone battery. If both of you live in the same apartment, our date night couples phone games list covers the co located version. This post is for the other case.

Below are nine games built for two phones on two separate couches. Each one survives a jittery call, an iOS version mismatch, and a three hour time zone gap. The ranking goes laughter per minute first, intimacy second, trivia dead last. One of the picks is ShakeGasm, because it was built in browser on purpose for sessions where neither person wants to wait on an app store download.

Why most couples games break at distance

A game designed for the same room assumes shared physical props. One phone passed between hands, one deck of cards on a coffee table, one speaker playing the timer. Over a video call that single phone pattern collapses in under three minutes. Lag turns a shake race into a staring contest. A shared card deck becomes a scramble to read a screenshot. The format breaks before round two.

The second failure mode is attention split. On an in person date night the phone is the game. On a call the phone is the game plus the connection plus the camera plus the lighting. Any format that demands fast thumb input while watching a face on a four inch window will lose to the format that lets both players settle into one screen each. The games below respect that constraint.

The third problem is intimacy fatigue. Long distance couples have already poured hours into talk based rituals. Another questions deck, another check in, another trivia block and the Sunday call starts feeling like a performance review. The nine picks hit different muscles so the call does not become one more conversation.

Nine games that hold up over FaceTime

Ordered from quietest co op to loudest shake chaos. Pick two from the list for a Tuesday, four for a slow Sunday, run them back to back, stop opening the one that flops.

  1. ShakeGasm with a shared timer. Free in the browser on both phones through app.shakegasm.com. Each player hits the same shake start seconds apart, screenshots the score to the chat, three rounds take six minutes, loser owes a voice note dare the next morning. The brand built it to run in browser so the ten second iOS install blocker never lands.
  2. Marco Polo video messages, question twist. Each partner records a sixty second clip asking three questions about the other person. The reply video answers plus three new ones. Not live, which is the point when the five hour time gap kicks in.
  3. Caribu or shared sketch canvas. Both phones on the same drawing board, one draws a prompt in thirty seconds, the other guesses in real time. Latency helps because the guess arrives half a second late and you watch it click.
  4. Chess or Backgammon on Chess.com plus voice call. The classic engine behind long distance couples who have run a daily game since 2004. Ten minutes a round, slow enough for voice to run alongside without stepping on moves.
  5. Teleparty or Scener movie sync. Ninety minute film you both have on the same platform, synced start, reactions into the sidebar as it plays. Pause rule, either person can pause once per act.
  6. Two Dots or Threes long distance co op. Each player clears the same level on their own phone, screenshots the final board to the chat, first to clear buys the next pizza.
  7. Spyfall online lobby, two player table. One role each, pretend to be in different cities while being in different cities, which is the funniest part of the game.
  8. Heads Up through the shared camera. One partner holds the phone to their forehead on video, the other reads the clue list from their own phone. Five minute rounds, high laughs per minute, works on hotel wifi.
  9. One question a night from a questions deck. Not thirty questions, one. Pick the app, agree the rule, close the app after the single answer. Compound effect at three months.

How to set up the call so it does not feel like Zoom

Long distance couples run a weekly call out of habit and the habit kills the magic by month four. The call is the container, the game is the content, and both have to work. Five adjustments collapse the Zoom fatigue feeling to zero. One lamp each, both off center behind the phone, with no ceiling light on either side. Phones on do not disturb for the full window, both devices, not silent, because silent still leaks through on iOS 18. One drink poured before the call starts, agreed ahead of time, no refills mid round. Audio over earbuds for both, not phone speaker, because reflex games need low latency audio and the speaker loop doubles the game sound.

The fifth adjustment is a hard end time stated at the start of the call, forty five minutes from the first game. Without it the call drifts past the game and into logistics, and the game stops feeling like play. End the window with a kiss emoji, close the app, and the next session arrives wanted. The games above expect a short window and they punish any session that bleeds past an hour. Couples who run the loop for a quarter report the Sunday 9pm slot becomes the most anticipated forty five minutes of the week, which beats the three hour drifting call every time.

Stakes that cross a time zone

A game without stakes becomes a scheduled check in. Stakes that work for a long distance couple do not require physical presence. The five below have each been tested in the wild and get opened more than once.

  • Loser writes a six sentence voice note the next morning about something the winner said last week.
  • Loser orders dinner delivered to the winner, through the winner's own food app, within an hour of game end.
  • Loser books the next flight date, including the airport pickup and the first dinner spot.
  • Loser gets vetoed on the next three song picks for the shared playlist.
  • Loser pulls a dare from the shared Notes doc. Build the doc on the first call with ten prompts each, number them, pick blind with a random number site.

The food delivery stake is the one most couples underrate. A pepperoni pizza arriving at the right apartment at 10:14pm, paid for by someone in a city six hours away, is a different kind of love letter than a text. The dare doc compounds the same way the in person dare jar for date night does, so after a quarter you have sixty private dares and a call window that fills itself with new material. Pair that with a free shake round on ShakeGasm at the top of the call and the energy curve of the whole hour tilts upward.

A fifteen minute weekly loop

Full nine game lists get read once and never run. A fifteen minute loop gets run every week. The format below has been tested by couples running across six time zones, from Lisbon to Mexico City to Jakarta. The structure is three games, three stakes, no small talk about work.

  1. One shake round of ShakeGasm, three shakes each, loser owes a voice note the next morning.
  2. One two minute drawing round on Caribu or any shared sketch tool, loser picks the next movie sync film.
  3. One Heads Up five card round through the shared camera, loser orders the next delivery.

Fifteen minutes, three laughs, three stakes. That is the whole call. Everything else fits around it. Research on long distance couples at the Gottman Institute shows weekly ritual beats daily effort for relationship stability, and the ritual works best when it has a shape.
