Anniversary Phone Games: 9 Picks for Night In

The reservation canceled 40 minutes out, the babysitter still arrived, and now you have three hours on a couch with the person you married. This is the anniversary most couples get in practice, not the one in the Instagram carousel. A phone game solves it in under two minutes of setup, costs between zero and six dollars, and skips the small talk you already did at breakfast. The trick is picking one that fits the specific year, not grabbing whatever is on the home screen.

Below are nine phone games tested on anniversaries from year one to year nineteen, sorted by what they do rather than how many five star reviews they collected. One is pure reflex, three are question based, two are dare based, one is a couples quiz, one is a video call hybrid for travel years, and one is a build your own jar. If you want a deeper lane on softer evenings, our couples phone games for date night roundup covers the broader category. For post game wind down, pillow talk phone games for 2am handles the hour after this one.

When the reservation falls through, the night does not have to

Most canceled reservations hit between 5pm and 7pm on a Friday or Saturday. That window is the strongest slot for a phone game, because both of you are already dressed, already hungry, and already slightly annoyed. Order delivery inside eight minutes, pour something, and start the first game before the food arrives. A 20 minute first round carries you to the doorbell without that weird stare into the TV. Keep the first pick light, save the spicy one for after dinner when the wine has done its work.

Anniversary year matters more than people admit. Year one couples want questions that test how much they remember from the first six months. Year seven couples want prompts that break the autopilot. Year fifteen couples want something that makes them laugh at a story they have told forty times. Match the game to the year, not to a generic best of list.

The 9 phone games for anniversary night in

Ranked by setup friction and how often the couples we tested with asked for a second round. Prices are in USD, accurate for November 2026.

  1. We Not Me (iOS and Android, free with a six dollar deep pack). A couples quiz that scores how well you predict each other's answers. Best for year three to year seven, when you think you know and the score reveals you do not. Setup takes 90 seconds. The loser owes a 10 minute foot rub.
  2. ShakeGasm (browser, free, no install). Pure shake reflex on one phone, two thumbs. The physical motion is the game, which means no reading, no cards, no setup past the URL. Couples Mode with built in spicy dares is in early dev. For now, pair it with a dare jar you wrote yourself. The loser of each round owes one dare pulled from the jar, which is why this pick goes in the top three for anniversaries where you want to skip talking and start touching.
  3. The And Couples Edition (iOS, five dollars). Deep question prompts designed by a filmmaker who recorded 400 couples on camera. Best for year one and year two, because the questions pry open the parts of the relationship you have not had to defend yet. One round runs 25 minutes if you let it.
  4. Kindu (iOS and Android, free with a four dollar pro). Matches you and your partner on intimate ideas you both say yes to, privately. Nobody sees the other's no. Ideal for year five and up, when both of you have ideas you have not said out loud. Pair it with the dare jar from pick two.
  5. Gottman Card Decks (iOS and Android, free). Research backed prompts from the Gottman Institute, 14 decks covering date night, open ended questions, salsa, and sex. Not sexy on its own, but the Salsa deck lands on anniversaries where the goal is to reconnect before anything else.
  6. Couply (iOS and Android, free with a seven dollar premium). A relationship app with daily challenges and a quiz library. Best used as a pre dinner warmup rather than the main event, because the UI is slow for a 10 minute round.
  7. Paired (iOS and Android, free with a five dollar monthly). Daily couple questions, swipe based, with a streak tracker. Travel anniversary pick when one of you is on a plane and the other is at home.
  8. House Party Trivia Couples (iOS and Android, free). Head to head trivia with a couples pack of 200 questions covering pop culture, first dates, and oddly specific milestone years. Loud, fast, good for year ten and up when a quiet night feels too quiet.
  9. Build your own jar on Notes app (free, five minutes). Open the Notes app, write 20 prompts, assign five easy ones, 10 medium, five hot. Shake the phone to randomize. This is what most couples end up back at by anniversary four, because nothing in the App Store knows your inside jokes.

How to pick the right game for your anniversary year

Year one through three couples are still in the recall phase, where the first date, the first apartment, and the first fight are inside the conversation window. Pick a question game, pick a prompt game, pick anything that lets one of you say the thing you have been meaning to say. We Not Me, The And, and the Gottman Open Ended deck hit this year hard. Year four through eight hit the autopilot wall, which is where Kindu, the dare jar, and ShakeGasm with your own prompt list break the script. Year nine and up need novelty, which is where the House Party Trivia couples pack and travel video games earn their place.

A practical rule after 12 anniversaries of testing: pick one quiet game and one loud game for the night. Start quiet before dinner, go loud after. Flipping that order kills the mood by minute 20 because the adrenaline from the loud round makes the quiet question feel like homework. For anniversaries where one of you is sick or exhausted, skip the loud round entirely and run two quiet games back to back, 20 minutes each.

What makes an anniversary phone game land

Three things separate an anniversary phone game from a generic couples game. Setup under two minutes, because the mood dies in the App Store. A stake attached to each round, even a small one, because couples who play without stakes end the round and scroll Instagram. A natural off ramp, meaning the game resolves in under 25 minutes so you can move on to dinner, dessert, or bed without the game dragging. Any pick above that fails on one of these three gets dropped after one anniversary.

The deeper mechanic is friction cost. A game that takes seven minutes to set up has to deliver a 40 minute reward to justify the install. A game that loads in 20 seconds only needs to deliver 10 good minutes to earn its slot. That is why the Notes app dare jar and ShakeGasm keep showing up near the top of this list across five test cycles. Both start in under 30 seconds. For couples who want a sharper edge to the dare jar, our dirty would you rather questions set plugs directly in.

Anniversary rule, year one through twenty: pick the game that starts in under two minutes, not the one with the best reviews.

The three anniversary stakes that work

Stakes decide whether a game ends at round one or runs for four rounds. Three stake structures land across every year we tested.

  • The 10 minute foot rub, which works because it continues the physical contact without demanding escalation. Useful on tired anniversaries and after a big dinner.
  • The one dare from the jar, which works because the loser has agency over which dare they draw. Keeps the mood playful even when the dare lands on the hot tier.
  • The partner picks the next game, which works because the loser still gets a say in the night. Eliminates the dead silence that happens after a one sided round.

Avoid money stakes, loser does dishes stakes, and anything that extends past the night. Anniversaries that end with a chore list on the fridge are the ones couples remember wrong.
