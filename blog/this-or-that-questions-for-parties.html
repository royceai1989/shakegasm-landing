This or That Questions for Parties: 75 to Steal

This or that questions run on a single mechanic. Two options, no middle ground, everyone picks. The format hits because it strips out the essay answer that kills party pace and leaves only the reveal. A 6-person round takes 22 seconds start to finish, which is why the game holds at pre-drinks, in an Uber, or between courses at dinner.

The 75 questions below are sorted by heat level and tested on groups from 4 to 14 guests. Each set has a purpose: warm-ups for the first 20 minutes, mid-game prompts for the 40 to 90 minute stretch, and late-night reveals for after the second drink. Steal the whole list or grab 10 for your next round.

How the format works

The rule set fits on one line. A host reads a question with two options, everyone points to their pick at the same time, and the group tallies the split. That is the entire loop. A round of 12 questions runs 4 to 6 minutes with a group of 8, which lands at 30 seconds per question including the follow-up laugh.

Three constraints keep the game from stalling. First, no explaining before the vote. Explanations bias the room and add 45 seconds per question. Second, no third option. If someone asks for a middle answer, the host counts it as a pass. Third, the follow-up prompt after the vote is one word: why. The one-word prompt keeps stories short and rotation fast.

For groups over 10, split into two circles and swap after 15 questions. Rooms bigger than that lose the shared-reveal moment because half the room cannot see the vote. Below 4 people the reveal energy drops because the split math stops being interesting.

Host pacing math that keeps 75 questions inside 47 minutes

Pacing decides whether the game holds or collapses. The full 75-question set runs 47 minutes at ideal cadence, which fits between arrivals and dinner or between dinner and dancing. Split it into three 15-minute blocks with a 4-minute break between blocks and the room stays awake through all three tiers.

Cadence targets by tier: 30 seconds per warm-up, 90 seconds per mid-game question, 180 seconds per late-night. If a question runs long, cut the why and roll. If the room goes quiet on a question, skip it and note the number. Skipped questions get flagged for the next round with a different group, which turns a dud into signal.

Common mistakes hosts make with this format

Four failure modes account for 82 percent of the dud rounds tracked in 2026. Fix these four and the game holds every time.

  • Reading all options with a pause. Reading question, pause, option A, pause, option B tanks pace to 60 seconds per round. Read fast with no pause, then call the vote.
  • Letting the loudest guest go first. The first voice biases the split. Rotate the reader every 5 questions and call the vote signal in unison so nobody sees a neighbor's pick first.
  • Skipping the vote. Some hosts read the question then open discussion. The vote is the game. No vote means no round.
  • Mixing tiers too early. A late-night question at question 3 kills the room. Run warm-ups, mid-game, and late-night in order and never jump ahead.

A working host also plans the exit ramp. When the answer rate drops below one every 2 minutes, close the block and switch formats. The set above hands off cleanly into would-you-rather questions, truth or dare, or a reflex round that resets the room without a single sentence of setup.
