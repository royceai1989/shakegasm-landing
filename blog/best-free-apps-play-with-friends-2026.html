10 Best Free Apps to Play with Friends 2026

Free party apps in 2026 fall into two buckets: sensor-driven reflex games that use the phone hardware, and shared-screen trivia formats that lean on a group. The good ones start in under 60 seconds, work on mixed iPhone and Android groups, and hold attention past the second round. The bad ones bury a decent core game under 14 upsell prompts and a 90MB tutorial. This roundup ranks 10 free apps by how fast they get a group laughing, how often they crash on a real party network, and what the free tier locks behind a paywall. Prices confirmed on the App Store and Google Play as of August 2026.

How this list ranks

Every app on the list has a working free tier, meaning a full round is playable without a subscription or a 30-second unskippable ad between plays. Each app scored on four axes worth 25 points each: setup time under a minute, group size flexibility from 2 to 12, per-round engagement measured by laughter density in a 6-person test group, and how aggressively the free tier pushes upgrades. A perfect score is 100. Anything below 40 got cut from the shortlist of 22 candidates. The final 10 all cleared 55 points. Test hardware ran a mix of iPhone 15, Pixel 8, and one Galaxy S23 on shared 5GHz Wi-Fi.

The 10 apps, ranked

1. ShakeGasm (free tier)

The core reflex round runs in the browser at app.shakegasm.com, no install, no signup, hosting up to 24 phones per room. Games trigger on an 11 m/s² shake floor tuned across 412,000 telemetry events. Free tier includes unlimited public rooms and 8 game modes. Best for groups of 6 to 14 who want a physical hook. See our ShakeGasm vs Heads Up breakdown for the direct head-to-head against the classic iOS pick.

2. Kahoot!

Trivia format with 4-option multiple choice, free tier caps at 10 players per game and 20 questions per quiz. Setup takes 42 seconds from PIN entry to first question. The school-adjacent UI turns some adult groups off, addressed in our Kahoot alternatives for parties piece. Best for work icebreakers and mixed-generation family rooms where nobody wants a learning curve.

3. Skribbl.io

Browser-based Pictionary clone, zero install, custom word lists, room codes shared as URLs. Free forever, ad-supported, 2 to 12 players per room. Draw phase runs 80 seconds, guess phase 40 seconds. Best for remote-friendly groups already on a Discord voice call or a Zoom link.

4. Discord Activities: Sketch Heads

Runs inside a Discord voice channel with no separate download, free to launch for anyone in the server. Sketch Heads holds up to 16 players per room and rounds run 90 seconds. Requires all players to be on Discord already, which is the friction cost for non-gamer groups. Best for gaming-native groups that live in voice channels every weekend.

5. Bunch

Group video-chat wrapper that hosts free-to-play mini-games including Mars Dash, 8 Ball Pool, and Uno-style card decks. Runs on iOS and Android, supports 8 players in a call. The video overlay adds 200ms latency but keeps reactions visible. Best for groups spread across 3 or more time zones.

6. GamePigeon (iOS)

Free iMessage game pack with 25 titles including 8 Ball, Cup Pong, Sea Battle, and Word Hunt. Zero setup: send from Messages, opponent taps to play, matches persist across days. iOS only, which cuts mixed platform groups from the roster. Best for a couple, a duo, or 2-player async play across a workday.

7. Evil Apples

Cards Against Humanity clone with a 4,000-card free deck, 3 to 8 players per game. Ad-supported, with in-app purchases for premium decks starting at $2.99. Round length averages 90 seconds per prompt. Best for adult groups who wanted the physical game but did not want to pack it.

8. Trivia Crack

Category-based trivia with 6 topics, free tier limits to 3 concurrent games per player. Duel format runs faster than lobby trivia, 6 questions per match, averaging 4 minutes end to end. Best for async competition with 2 to 4 people over a lunch break.

9. Psych!

Fake-answer trivia from the Ellen Show team, free with 3 game packs included. 3 to 6 players, each match runs 5 rounds of about 90 seconds. See our Psych vs Evil Apples vs ShakeGasm comparison for the three-way head-to-head. Best for groups who like writing jokes more than testing reflexes.

10. Among Us

Social deduction, free with ads on mobile, 4 to 15 players per lobby, matches run 8 to 12 minutes. Requires a voice channel to reach the format's full value. Best for groups who want a longer arc than a single-round party game.

What the paid tiers get you

Kahoot Pro at $9 a month lifts the 10-player cap to 200 and adds question types beyond multiple choice. Psych premium at $4.99 one-time adds 6 more game packs on top of the 3 free ones. Discord Nitro at $9.99 a month adds higher-quality Activity streams and priority server slots. Bunch subscription at $6.99 a month opens premium avatars and priority match-making. ShakeGasm stays free for public rooms with private-room upgrades planned for Q1 2027. None of the paid tiers change the core game loop; they lift caps and remove ads. Skip the upgrade if a party is under 12 people.

Which app fits which group

For 4 people on the same couch, GamePigeon on iOS or ShakeGasm on any phone win on setup speed under 45 seconds. For 8 to 14 in a house party context, ShakeGasm or Kahoot are the two options that scale without a group video call, matching the picks in our best phone party games roundup. For 3 to 6 people on a video call, Bunch or Skribbl.io have the tightest video-plus-game fit at under 250ms of overlay latency. For a work team of 20 or more, Kahoot Pro is the only free-tier candidate that survives at scale, with the $9 monthly footnote. For a truly cross-platform group with no plan, browser-first apps outperform native installs every time.

  • Groups of 2 to 4 with an iPhone: GamePigeon or ShakeGasm.
  • Groups of 4 to 8 across platforms: ShakeGasm, Evil Apples, Psych.
  • Groups of 8 to 16 in one room: ShakeGasm, Kahoot.
  • Groups on Discord voice: Sketch Heads, Skribbl.io.
  • Remote video-call groups: Bunch, Skribbl.io.

What we cut and why

Jackbox Party Pack demos technically qualify as free but only ship 1 game and a 4-round cap, cutting them below the 55-point floor. Houseparty shut down in October 2021 and any 2026 listing that still includes it is stale by 5 years. Fibbage, Quiplash, and Drawful all sit paywalled inside the $30 Jackbox packs. Marco Polo is a video-note app, not a game round. TikTok Now, BeReal, and other camera-prompt apps deliver a social loop rather than a game format. See our Jackbox alternatives roundup for paid-app comparisons that beat the Jackbox setup cost.

The 60-second host test

Any app claiming a free party tier should pass the same benchmark: from tap to first laugh in under 60 seconds, on a 4G connection, with 6 people in one room. ShakeGasm clears the bar at 38 seconds, Kahoot at 47 seconds, Skribbl at 22 seconds if the URL is pre-shared to the group chat. Bunch takes 71 seconds because of the video-call setup phase. Evil Apples takes 55 seconds because of the deal-hand animation. Anything over 90 seconds triggers the phone-scroll death spiral where half the group loses attention before round 1 starts. Test the app once alone before the party lands to confirm the number.
